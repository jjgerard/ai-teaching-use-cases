Our Approach to Academic Integrity
BIMM University has adopted the Quality Assurance
Agency's Academic Integrity Charter for UK
Higher Education. 2 The Charter sets out the following principles:
This Charter represents the collective commitment of the UK higher education sector to promote
academic integrity and take action against academic misconduct.
Students who commit academic misconduct, especially if they deliberately cheat, risk their
academic and future careers. The implications, however, go far wider than higher education. It is
a societal issue. Graduates could enter the workforce without the necessary skills, knowledge and
competency, with potential public health and safety implications.

Appeals Against Decisions on Academic Misconduct
Students have the right to appeal against decisions concerning academic misconduct on the
following grounds:
•

You experienced circumstances that affected your assessment, but there was a good reason why
the Assessment Board was not aware of them when it made its decision. For example, this may
include illness, bereavement, or other serious personal circumstances t
hat could not reasonably
have been reported earlier.

•

There is evidence that the correct procedures were not followed, and this may have affected the
outcome of the decision. This could include administrative errors, failure to follow University
regulations, or other mistakes in the assessment process.

•

There is evidence that bias, prejudice, or unfair treatment may have influenced the marking
process, moderation process, or Assessment Board's decision.

Appeals against academic misconduct decisions are considered in the same way as an academic
appeal, under the Student Appeals Procedure . This procedure sets out the appeals process,
deadlines, evidence requirements, review stage, and your right to seek an independent review by the
Office of the Independent Adjudicator (OIA) once the University's procedures have been completed.
