All use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) must follow the University or your placement's guidance.

AI tools must not be used to generate your assessment and submit it as if it was your own work.

- Student Guidance: Your assignment briefs will state if you are allowed to use AI and what types of use are permissible. You must follow those instructions to comply with the University or your placement's regulations. When permitted to use AI, it is your responsibility to keep a record of the prompts used, and drafts of your work to evidence that the final submission is your own work.

- Researcher Guidance: As well as following the University’s AI Postgraduate Researchers (PGR) guidance, researchers should also check publishers' policies about how and when AI can be used when writing for publication.

- Staff Guidance: Advice and additional resources to help academic staff navigate using Generative AI in their teaching practice.

Before Using AI

Before using an AI tool, consider the following:

- Do you have permission from the University or your placement?

- Will the AI tool help you with your learning?

- Can you verify its content?

- Are you keeping to the principles of academic integrity?

- Are you always in control of its output?

What is AI?

AI is a branch of data science and works through programming algorithms built on probability mathematics. AI is a broad field that incorporates many different areas of computing and machine learning. Initial discoveries started as early as the mid-1950s.

The term can refer to many different types of tools which perform different functions.

For example:

- Forecasting and prediction - e.g. weather patterns and financial markets

- Anomaly detection models - e.g. medical imaging, face recognition

- Recommendation systems - e.g. content recommendations from Netflix and social media platforms

- Generative AI large language models and image generation - e.g. ChatGPT, CoPilot, Midjourney

- Search tool - e.g. Research Rabbit, Perplexity

Learn more through Microsoft’s Artificial intelligence 101

Generative AI

Generative (AI), often referred to as Gen AI, are web-based tools that use algorithms, data, and statistical models to draw reasonable inferences to generate content of its own. These tools rely on large amounts of training data to generate its content using natural language that gives the appearance of a human response. This content can include text, images, video, or code.

It is important to remember that however real the response, it is the outcome of probability maths and programming. There is no wider understanding of the world or the context, and this leads to many of the errors Generative AI produces.

Learn more through Google Cloud’s Introduction to Generative AI

Examples of how AI can support you in your study?

Inspiring

- Generate research questions from topic ideas

- Generate prompts to improve your use of AI tools

- Suggest advanced search strategies and Boolean terms

Planning and Management

- Suggest titles, structures, subheadings, or themes for projects

- Break down tasks or build a study timetable

- Act as a debating partner to challenge your argument

Sources and data collection

- Recommend sources, articles, or data sets

- Give general information to aid understanding

- Generate questionaires for data collection

Summarising and Consolidating

- Summarise lecture notes or journal articles

- Explain complex theories in simpler language

- Summarise video transcripts or other multimedia

Analysing

- Help identify themes, patterns, and motifs in data sets

- Support self-reflection and critical thinking

- Analyse content in videos

Composing

- Generate images, text, music, or programme code

- Compose Excel formulas

- Support creative tasks like brainstorming ideas

Translating

- Translate small sections of your written or recorded work

- Translate commonly used non-English terms e.g. Ibid.

- Suggest translations of common phrases and idioms

Presenting data

- Turn data into charts, graphs, or tables

- Create slide templates for presentations

- Suggest effective visual layouts for presentations

Checking and proofreading

- Spot errors and suggest corrections in your writing

- Provide alternative phrasing

- Offer feedback on drafts

Other

- This list is non-exhaustive and AI will develop and change over time.

- See our Example Prompts of different AI uses.

Disclaimer

When using AI for assessments, it is your responsibility to demonstrate that you have completed the assignment yourself. You may be required to provide evidence of this such as:

- Version-controlled documents

- Draft submissions

- Research notes

- Planning documents

- Data files

- Developmental journals or sketchbooks

- Source materials and annotated references)

You are also required to acknowledge where and how you have used generative AI in an assignment, to aid this it is a good idea keep a record of:

- The prompts used and the AI output

- How you incorporated the response in your assignment

Also, be careful when using AI that you abide by copyright restrictions.
