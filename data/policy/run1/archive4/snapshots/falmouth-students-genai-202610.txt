Academic integrity and why it matters

Academic integrity is a set of values that lies at the heart of university life. These fundamental values are "honesty, trust, fairness, respect and responsibility in scholarship" (Falmouth University Academic Integrity Policy). To comply with these principles, your work should be:

- Correctly and fully referenced

- Produced by you, and only you

- Original and unique

- Honest and trustworthy

Why does academic integrity matter?

At university, you are part of a wider academic community. Whether you're writing an essay, creating a piece of art, composing music, or designing a digital game, you're drawing on a network of information, influences and inspirations. Academic integrity is what allows this exchange to work fairly and transparently.

Academic work, here at Falmouth University, is about developing your own perspective through research and practice. This means engaging with what others have said, seen, made, or written; and bringing these influences into your own work. Referencing is how you do that with integrity. It's a system that shows clearly when you're using someone else's work, and it helps build credibility by linking your ideas to a wider conversation.

So, properly acknowledging your sources by consistently and accurately referencing and citing them demonstrates academic integrity.

Academic Misconduct

Universities trust you to be honest about the work you submit and will ask you to include a signed statement of academic integrity with your assignment submissions. If your work is found to have broken these principles, you may be accused of academic misconduct.

One form of academic misconduct is plagiarism. This means using someone else's work, ideas, or words without proper acknowledgment. Most universities, including Falmouth, use text-matching software such as Turnitin to help them detect copied work. In addition to plagiarism, academic misconduct also includes acts of collusion, commissioning, duplication (including self-plagiarism), and misrepresentation.

All students are recommended to read the University's Academic Integrity Policy where these are explained. You should also be aware of the Academic Misconduct Procedure.

The recent widespread availability of Generative AI tools has complicated discussion around academic integrity. Gen AI tools can potentially erase, or at least blur, some of the boundaries higher education has historically used to define "your work". Make sure you read the Student Guide to Using AI in Academic Work. If you are unsure, talk to your course team.
