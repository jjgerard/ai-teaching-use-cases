Artificial Intelligence (AI)

You have probably come across artificial intelligence (AI) in more ways than you realise, from facial recognition on your phone to personalised playlists and smart assistants.

AI is becoming a regular part of everyday life, so it is important that you understand what generative AI is, how you can use it, and what its benefits and limitations are.

Generative AI

Generative AI (Gen AI) refers to tools that can create content, like text, images, code, or even music, based on the instructions you give it. These tools use something called machine learning to spot patterns in huge amounts of data. By analysing this data, generative AI can respond to your prompts with relevant and often creative outputs, using models trained on language, images, and more.

As a student, you will likely come across AI-powered tools in your studies, whether it is for research, writing support, or creative projects. These platforms are designed to be easy to use, with simple interfaces that let you interact with the AI without needing any technical background.

While generative AI can be incredibly helpful, it is not perfect. It is still developing and it is essential to think critically about the information it gives you and how you can use it in your academic work.

Strengths of using AI

- Efficiency: AI tools can save you time.

- Accessibility: AI can support different learning styles, offering explanations in simpler language or generating visual aids.

- Idea generation: great for getting started or exploring different perspectives on a topic.

- Writing support: AI can help with grammar, spelling, and sentence structure by suggesting alternatives.

- Data handling: some tools can help you analyse data, create charts, or write basic code.

Limitations of using AI

- Accuracy: AI can sound confident but still give you incorrect or misleading information. Always fact-check.

- Lack of critical thinking: AI does not understand context or nuance the way you do, it cannot replace your own analysis or judgement.

- Bias and gaps: AI is trained on existing data, which can include biases or outdated views.

- Over-reliance: it is a tool, not a replacement for learning. Relying too much on AI can limit your development of key academic skills.

- Academic Integrity: Using AI to do your work for you (e.g. writing full essays) may breach university policies. Always check with your tutor.
