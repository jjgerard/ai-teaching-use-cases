Guidance for Students on the Use of Generative Artificial
Intelligence Tools in Education
Regularly updated: last update 15 September 2025. Comments? Email cad@abdn.ac.uk.

The University is committed to supporting staff and students to use generative artificial intelligence
(GenAI) tools in a responsible, critical, and ethical manner. GenAI tools can be used to generate
various types of new content, including images, text, music, and videos. Examples of GenAI tools
include, but are not limited to: MS Copilot, ChatGPT, DALL-E, DeepSeek, GitHub Copilot, Google
Gemini, Midjourney and Perplexity AI.
GenAI offers immense potential, from boosting creativity to improving writing skills. However, these
tools can generate misleading or incorrect information and can negatively impact on your learning.
You can find out more about what GenAI is through our toolkit resources.
Important: You must not use GenAI to do your critical thinking, analysis, evaluation, or
synthesis. Neither should you use it to generate essays, poems, or any creative works.
The purpose of these assignments is to demonstrate your unique ideas and creativity.
Remember, using GenAI tools to generate content for work submitted for assessment, without
authorisation or acknowledgement, will be regarded as academic misconduct and investigated as a
plagiarism offence under the Code of Practice on Student Discipline (Academic). You can find out
more by watching our introductory video.

1. Limitations and Ethical Considerations When Using GenAI Tools
Some of the limitations and ethical considerations associated with using GenAI tools in your studies
are described below.
Important: You must understand the limitations and ethical considerations associated
with using GenAI tools in your studies if you are to develop the skills to use these tools
responsibly, critically, and ethically.

1.1 Inaccurate information and misinterpretation of users’ prompts
GenAI tools generate content by predicting the next plausible word or section of programming code
based on patterns in the data it has been trained on. They do not get the underlying message or
understand and therefore the output can be inaccurate or reflect incorrect information and biases
inherent in the data it was trained on. They can therefore confidently fabricate content, as well as
generate fake citations and references. This is a phenomenon known as “hallucinations”. In addition,

What you can do: Ensure you critically evaluate the outputs from GenAI tools and use

of prompts and assess the quality of the outputs. Further support on developing your
information skills is available.

1.2 Privacy and data considerations
There are risks to data privacy and intellectual property when entering prompts or data into GenAI
tools. Always ensure that you have the appropriate permissions in place before entering any data
into these tools. Please note that any learning materials (e.g. lectures) and assessment information
(e.g. questions, tasks, activities) must not be entered into these tools without the explicit permission
of the author (e.g. Course Coordinator, Lecturer).
What you can do: Never enter personal and sensitive data into GenAI tools. Review
their privacy policy and terms of use, or equivalent statements, to inform your use.
Further support on information security is available.

1.3 Ethical considerations
If you are going to be using GenAI tools, you must take the following into consideration:
•
•

•

Perpetuating harmful stereotypes – since GenAI models inherit biases from their training data
it is essential to identify and correct these biases in any outputs that you then use to prevent
the perpetuation of harmful stereotypes.
Environmental impact – since GenAI models consume significant electricity and water, often
linked to data centres, reflect on how you use these tools to minimise your impact. For
example, write clear, precise prompts to reduce repeated queries and avoid asking for the
generation of multiple versions of the same output (image, text, video) in order to choose
one.
Human impact – use providers that share their employment practices and are committed to
fair wages and good working conditions.
What you can do: Reflect on your use of these tools – can you minimize your
environmental or human impact? Have you cross-checked with other sources before
accepting and sharing the output?

1.4 Copyright and plagiarism
GenAI tools can generate new content from content produced by people (e.g. artists, musicians,
writers, scientists) without acknowledging them, which is regarded as plagiarism.
What you can do: Review the data the GenAI tool has been trained on (is the dataset
open source? Or a licensed dataset?) and reference appropriate sources even if the

2. How GenAI Tools Can Be Used to Support Your Learning
GenAI tools can be a useful learning aid, for example by summarising complex concepts in a form that
you may find easier to understand. However, when it comes to work submitted for assessment, it is
important that the work is entirely your own. GenAI tools, if authorised by the Course Coordinator,
can aid the process of completing an assignment, but they should not be used as a means of
generating the assignment (or part of the assignment) and submitted as if it were your own work. The
latter is regarded as academic misconduct.
You would do yourself a disservice by outsourcing your work to a GenAI tool as you will not develop
the requisite level of knowledge, skills and understanding needed to succeed in University and in your
future career.
Below are some suggestions on how GenAI tools can support your learning:
•
•
•
•
•
•

Generating ideas, sparking your creativity
Drafting an outline structure (headings, bullet points)
Enhancing your writing skills by providing suggestions on alternative ways to communicate to
improve the coherence, style and grammar of your writing
Experimenting with tone, voice and style
Formatting a reference list by specifying the citation style you are using (e.g. APA, MLA
Chicago, Harvard) though staff and students do have access to ProQuest Refworks
Generating summary explanations for complex concepts, being mindful that inaccurate
information can be generated
Important: GenAI tools can be powerful aids, but they are a poor substitute to
developing your own academic abilities. View GenAI tools as a means to enhance your
learning journey, not replace it.

3. Your Responsibilities When Submitting Work for Assessment
When submitting work for assessment it is your responsibility to:
•
•
•
•
•

Adhere to the assessment guidelines specified by your Course Coordinator. Ensure you check
your course guide and assessment instructions for guidance on the use of GenAI tools in work
submitted for assessment. If in doubt about their use ask your Course Coordinator.
If your Course Coordinator has made it clear that you can use GenAI tools in work submitted
for assessment, ensure you understand to what extent these tools can be used.
Ensure it is your own original work, embracing the values of academic integrity (honesty,
trust, fairness, respect, responsibility and courage), and avoiding the pitfalls of plagiarism,
collusion, contract cheating and data falsification.
Ensure you keep copies of drafts, notes, your workings, and any annotations or comments on
documents, so that you can show the development of your ideas and writing. Guidance on
assignment notes, drafts and document versioning is available.
Be prepared to demonstrate your understanding and explain your thinking, if asked to do so.

3.1 What happens if you are suspected of using GenAI tools inappropriately?
In instances where a staff member suspects that there are elements in your assessed work that have
been created using GenAI tools, and this usage has not been acknowledged or was explicitly
prohibited according to the assessment guidelines, they have the option to discuss this with you or
they may request that this be investigated further.
During this process, you may be required to:
Provide Evidence of the Creation Process
This evidence could include earlier drafts, notes or documentation that illustrates the
development of the final assessment. Guidance on assignment notes, drafts and document
versioning is available.
• Engage in a Discussion
You might be asked to engage in a discussion about your submission, to determine if your
level of knowledge aligns with that demonstrated in the submitted assignment.
•

It is your responsibility to ensure you can satisfy the staff member that the work you submitted for
assessment is your own original work. These measures are in place to ensure the integrity of the
assessment process and uphold academic standards.

4. How to Acknowledge the Use of GenAI Content
It is recommended that you provide:
•
•
•

The name(s) of the GenAI tool(s) used
A statement summarising how they were used
The date on which the content was generated

Consult the detailed guidance on acknowledging the use of GenAI tools for further information.

5. Support Available
While you can use GenAI tools to support and enhance your learning, don’t forget about our Student
Learning Service (SLS), which is there to help you enhance your academic skills including academic
writing, maths skills and study skills.
