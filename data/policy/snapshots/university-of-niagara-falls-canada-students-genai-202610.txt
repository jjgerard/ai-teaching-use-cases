1. Policy Statement
University of Niagara Falls Canada (UNF) strives to be at the forefront of innovation in
higher education. As a forward-looking Canadian university, we recognize artificial
intelligence (AI) as a transformative force in teaching, learning, research, and institutional
operations. We commit to fostering innovation that enhances educational quality,
strengthens research integrity, improves operational effectiveness, and prepares
graduates for a rapidly evolving professional landscape.
This policy reflects our dedication to ethical, responsible, and forward-looking
procurement, integration, and approved use or adoption of A.I. guided by the principles
of transparency, fairness, accountability, and privacy.
2. Purpose and Scope
The purpose of this A.I. Policy is to guide in the transformative potential of A.I..
technologies and the importance of ensuring that they are procured, integrated, used,
and adopted in a manner that respects individual rights, promotes societal well-being,
and fosters trust. This policy applies to all UNF students, faculty (full-time, sessional,
and contract course developers), and staff. It also applied to all contractors and thirdparty vendors providing AI-enabled services.
3. Mission and Goals
The mission of UNF is to foster innovation, facilitate information literacy, and enhance
the overall educational experience. The goals of the digital collection are to provide
accessible and diverse digital resources that support the research, teaching, and
learning needs of our academic community as well as our operational needs
4. Guiding Principles
All institutional AI adoption, use, integration, procurement, development and deployment
will be guided by the following principles:
a) Transparency
AI systems used in teaching, learning, research, and administration must be
disclosed where appropriate. Individuals have the right to know when AI materially
contributes to decisions affecting them.
b) Fairness
AI systems must be evaluated for bias and discriminatory impact prior to adoption or

staff, leadership, partners, and others.
c) Accountability
Humans oversight is essential. Humans must remain responsible for decisions
informed or supported by AI. High-risk automated decision-making without
human oversight is prohibited.
d) Privacy
All institutionally adopted AI tools must comply with Canadian privacy requirements
(e.g., PIPEDA) and institutional privacy policies. Data collection, storage, and
processing must be minimized and safeguarded. Collected, stored, or processed
data must be used ethically and responsibly.
5. Selection Criteria
A.I. technologies and tools will be selected based on their relevance to the curriculum,
scholarly significance, and alignment with the mission and goals of the library and the
institution. The following criteria will be considered during the selection process:
a. Academic and Research Significance
b. Currency and Timeliness
c. Authoritativeness and Credibility
d. Accessibility and Usability
e. Cost-effectiveness
f. Licensing and Copyright Compliance
6. UNF A.I. Responsibilities
The University is committed to the responsible and ethical use, adoption, procurement,
integration, development and deployment of A.I.. This includes but is not limited to:
1. Supporting the development of A.I. tools that align with the educational
objectives and values of the University.
2. Ensuring institutionally adopted A.I. technologies comply with relevant
privacy regulations and align with our commitments to transparency, fairness,
accountability, and privacy.
3. Regularly assessing the performance and impact of A.I. tools on teaching,
learning, and academic outcomes, and making necessary adjustments to
improve effectiveness and mitigate potential biases.
4. Providing training and support to faculty and staff on the appropriate
selection, use, adoption, and integration of A.I. technologies.
5. Collaborating with relevant stakeholders to ensure that A.I. development
aligns with best practices and ethical standards.
7. Collection Assessment
Regular assessments of institutionally supported A.I. tools will be conducted to evaluate
their effectiveness in meeting the needs of theinstituion. Feedback from faculty, staff, and
students will be actively sought, and adjustments to the collection will be made based on
usage patterns, evolving curriculum, and emerging trends.

8. Budget Allocation
Funding for AI procurement will be allocated based on the strategic priorities outlined in
this policy. The University will strive to maximize the value of available resources while
maintaining fiscal responsibility and transparency.
9. Revision of Policy
This Artificial Intelligence (A.I.) Policy will be reviewed annually to ensure its alignment
with the evolving needs of the institution, and advancements in AI technologies. Any
necessary revisions will be made in consultation with relevant stakeholders and in
accordance with established governance processes.

13. Responsibility
Academic Council
•
Provides final approval for institutionally supported AI technologies
recommended through established processes.
•
Ensures AI-related decisions align with academic standards, program quality,
and institutional priorities.
•
Reviews and approves revisions to this policy and related procedures.
Vice President, Academic (VPA)
•
Serves as the executive sponsor of this policy and oversees its
implementation across academic units.
•
Ensures that proposed AI technologies undergo the appropriate academic,
ethical, and governance review before procurement or approval for use.
•
Allocates or recommends resources to support institution-wide AI integration,
training, and evaluation.
•
Ensures alignment between AI adoption and the University’s academic
mission, strategic plan, and quality assurance requirements.
Information Technology (IT) Service
•
Conducts technical assessments of proposed AI technologies, including
security, integration feasibility, system performance, and data governance
implications.
•
Leads or collaborates in conducting Privacy Impact Assessments (PIAs) and
risk analyses in accordance with institutional privacy requirements and
applicable Canadian legislation.
•
Ensures that institutionally supported AI tools meet technical, cybersecurity,
and infrastructure standards.
•
Provides ongoing technical support for approved AI systems and maintains
documentation related to integrations, data flows, and risk mitigation.
Centre for Teaching Excellence
•
Reviews proposed AI technologies for pedagogical value, alignment with
evidence-based teaching practices, and accessibility considerations.
•
Advises on implications of AI adoption for teaching, learning, course design,
and academic integrity.
•
Provides faculty development, training, and support for the responsible,
ethical, and effective use of AI tools in teaching and learning contexts.
•
Collaborates with IT and the Library to evaluate AI tools that may impact
academic integrity, learning outcomes, or classroom practice.
Library
•
Evaluates AI technologies that support research, information literacy,
scholarly communication, and learning resources.
•
Provides guidance on licensing, copyright, data use agreements, and vendor
terms associated with AI tools.
•
Supports the academic community in understanding responsible use of AI for
research, information retrieval, and content generation.
