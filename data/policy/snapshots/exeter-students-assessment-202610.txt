                    GenAI Use in Assessments
This is a guide to the University of Exeter's AI in assessment guidance. 
If you are not sure what is expected for a particular assessment 
after reading your assessment brief and all of the information on this page
, please speak to your module lead before you complete the assessment. 
Please note: 
While this page may look different compared to the 2025/6 academic year, the AI in assessments guidance has remained the same for the 2026/7 academic year. We have only streamlined the content of this page for ease of understanding.
The University of Exeter follows a
 four-tier approach 
to GenAI use in assessments: 
AI-integrated
The use of GenAI tools is required by the module’s Intended Learning Outcomes.
Your assessment brief will include an explanation of how AI should (and should not) be used and a checklist of purposes for AI use.
You must include GenAI prompts and, where possible, hyperlinks to their output as part of your submission. You may like to use the 'record of GenAI use' template provided on the 
'Recording and Referencing GenAI' guide
. If hyperlinks are not produced through the tools you use, save the AI-generated output in a document that you can easily access later (e.g. on OneDrive). You may be asked to produce this material in the event of an academic conduct inquiry. 
You must never use AI to translate more than a word or short phrase into English unless agreed in the checked boxes in your assessment brief.
AI-assisted
You can choose to use GenAI tools ethically and responsibly to assist in the development of an assessment.
The assessment brief will include a checklist of purposes that AI can be used for.
If you choose to use GenAI tools in AI-assisted assessments, you must include GenAI prompts and, where possible, hyperlinks to their output as part of your submission. You may like to use the 'record of GenAI use' template provided on the 
'Recording and Referencing GenAI' guide
. If hyperlinks are not produced through the tools you use, save the AI-generated output in a document that you can easily access later (e.g. on OneDrive). You may be asked to produce this material in the event of an academic conduct inquiry. 
You must never use AI to translate more than a word or short phrase into English unless agreed in the checked boxes in your assessment brief.
AI-minimal
You may use AI tools for checking spelling and grammar mistakes only, with no other impact on the structure or content of the assessment. This includes AI tools such as Microsoft Word's grammar and spelling checker, and 
not
 GenAI tools such as Microsoft Copilot or ChatGPT. This is because using AI tools outside of these uses prevents fair assessment of your ability to achieve module learning outcomes. 
While completing AI-minimal assessments, you may find that some search tools you are using have GenAI features, such as source summarisation tools. Do not copy, paste or paraphrase information from these AI-generated summaries.
AI-prohibited
You must not use GenAI tools under any circumstances when preparing and writing for this assessment. This is because using GenAI tools prevents fair assessment of your ability to demonstrate module learning outcomes. 
Submitting your assessment
No matter what category of AI use your assessment is, when you finish your work, ask yourself: 'Who is the author of this work?'. Make sure you can confidently answer 'me' before you submit your assessment.
When you submit your assessment to ELE, you will need to check a box to confirm that you have complied with the University's policy on academic conduct, and with the expectations regarding the use of GenAI as specified in your assessment brief.  It will not be possible to submit your assessment without checking this box.
Further information

                    Key points to be aware of
If you are using a GenAI tool as part of your academic work, please check that you have followed this guidance:
A. If you are not sure whether and how you can use GenAI in your academic work, please check your assignment brief and discuss with your module lead.
B. You must never upload sensitive or identifying material to an AI tool.
C. You must never present material that has been generated by AI as your own work or the work of someone else.   
D. GenAI tools should not be used to generate content which is included in an assessment without following guidance on 
acknowledging and referencing GenAI
. This would be an offence under the University’s 
Academic Conduct and Practice policy
. 
E. It is important to note that GenAI tools do not always output 
accurate or unbiased information
. You must therefore 
critically analyse
 and verify any information that you plan to use in your academic work, including any references or sources provided by a GenAI tool. 
F. Ensure that you have the necessary skills to use AI critically and responsibly. Check out the University's '
AI: Understanding it'
 guide to develop your AI literacy.
G. Please be aware that practice and guidance in this area will change over time, so check the 
AI: Understanding it
 guide regularly for the University’s most up to date guidance.
                    Assistive Writing Technology
The University's guidelines on the use of GenAI in assessments appreciate that assistive technology using AI may have been recommended as part of the Disabled Students Allowance / Apprenticeship Needs Assessment Process. The majority of these tools are allowed even in AI-prohibited assignments. If there is any uncertainty with this please check with your Faculty Hub via SID: 
https://sid.exeter.ac.uk/
If you would like to discuss this further please engage with 
AccessAbility
. 
A note on Grammarly
: Please be aware that Grammarly has integrated GenAI functions. Some of these functions can be switched off, and others would require you to click on specific functions to use them. Please be aware that incorporating Grammarly's suggested edits into your academic work may breach the University's Academic Honesty policy. Find out how to turn off the AI function in Grammarly by reading the 
FAQs

                    Academic Honesty and Policy
Academic honesty
 requires:
Giving full credit to contributions (through proper referencing)
Never falsifying or fabricating the results of any research
Ensuring that all submitted work is a true representation of one’s own understanding.
While AI tools can support your learning, they must not replace your independent thought and original work in assessments. The University needs to assess your knowledge and skills to award degrees. 
The Academic Conduct and Practice policy of the 
TQA Manua
l clarifies that using AI-generated content should be acknowledged in the same way as that taken from human authors. Misuse of AI generated content can lead to a student being found guilty of one or more of the offences listed in section 12.3 of the policy. This could include: 
Plagiarism
which covers situations where a person deliberately or accidentally represents work or ideas as their own without appropriate acknowledgement or referencing, including AI; 
Misrepresentation
 includes misrepresenting an AI generated source as your own work;   
Fabrication 
(creation of false data/references) could also involve inaccurate material taken from AI tools;  
Falsification
 if AI is used to manipulate results in an unapproved way.
