1.

Introduction

South East Technological University is committed to the highest standards of scholarship
and aims to foster academic integrity in its students and graduates.
This policy is informed and aligns to the National Academic Integrity Network2 (NAIN)
publications: Academic Integrity: National Principles and Lexicon of Common Terms (2021,
2025) and Academic Integrity Guidelines (2021). Development of a culture of academic
integrity is ‘the responsibility of all stakeholders across the University’3 (ENAI (European
Network for Academic Integrity), 2018, as cited in QQI (Quality and Qualifications Ireland),
2021, p.3).
The policy itself is also informed by relevant national and international guidelines,
including Quality and Qualifications Ireland (QQI) Assessment and Standards (Revised
2022), the European Association for Quality Assurance in Higher Education (ENQA)
Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance in the European Higher Education Area
(2015), and the National Academic Integrity Network (NAIN) Academic Integrity
Guidelines (2021).

2.

Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to ensure and foster academic integrity and good academic
practice.
SETU supports excellence in all academic endeavours and commits to creating a culture and
learning environment in which students can excel in their academic achievement. SETU will
act responsibly and ethically to ensure the highest standards are maintained.
The integrity of academic standards is fundamental to upholding the reputation of the
University and, as such, SETU considers proven academic misconduct as a serious offence.
Students must not participate in behaviour or conduct which may be regarded as a breach
of the SETU Academic Misconduct Policy.
SETU promotes personal and professional development in all aspects of academic integrity.
Students are expected to act reasonably and to take responsibility for their behaviour and
conduct in academic matters. To that end, SETU expects all students to uphold the tenets of
the SETU Student Charter. Students at SETU should ensure that they, and where possible,
their fellow students, act in a manner that is appropriate and one which represents SETU at
the highest possible standard. Students are expected to inform themselves, by reading and
complying with relevant guidelines and policies. They should acknowledge and comply with
the Student Expected Behaviour Policy and Disciplinary Procedure throughout their studies

3.

Scope

This policy applies to all students of SETU who undertake activities to gain academic credit
and situations such as journal articles, conference presentations, competitions and practice
placement, where academic work may be submitted for recognition. This policy applies to all
Students of SETU, including while Students are on work/practice/industrial placement or
similar activities. “Student” means ‘any person who has accepted an offer to study and is
pursuing a course of study at SETU and includes any Visiting Student’. A “Visiting Student”
means ‘any Student who may also be bound by the terms and conditions of their contract (of
employment and/or the policies and procedures of their host institution) and are considered as
Students under SETU Regulations, Policies and Procedures to include this Policy’.

4.

Principles

Academic integrity is a key element in maintaining confidence in, and the reputation of, our
awards. It is fundamental to the ability, self-esteem and professionalism of our graduates.
The University commits to realising these ambitions for our students and for the wider
community, by adhering to the following principles:

4

4.1

Academic integrity is founded on the core values of mutual respect, honesty, trust,
courage, fairness, respect, and responsibility4.

4.2

Academic integrity is the responsibility of all university stakeholders.

4.3

Development of a culture of academic integrity will be supported by the University
through the provision of information, professional development opportunities and
other supports to all staff and students.

4.4

The University embeds academic integrity as a fundamental pillar for all curriculum
design and development activities, including all teaching, learning, assessment and
feedback strategies.

5.

Definitions

For the purpose of this policy:
Academic Integrity5 is defined as compliance with ethical and professional principles,
standards, practices, and a consistent system of values, that serves as guidance for making
decisions and taking actions in education, research, and scholarship.
Academic Misconduct6 is defined as behaviours perpetrated by individuals or institutions
that transgress ethical standards held in common between other individuals and/ or groups
in institutions of education, research, or scholarship.
Academic Misconduct includes: Unauthorised Content Generation (UCG), Academic
Dishonesty; Academic Misrepresentation; Academic Impropriety; Academic Cheating;
Academic Malpractice; Academic Violation; Academic Mispractice.
Academic Misconduct includes all actions which contravene Academic Integrity. These
include breaches of examination regulations, cheating, plagiarism, impersonation, purchase
of examination material, contract cheating, data falsification, misuse of generative artificial
intelligence and other acts which dishonestly use information to gain academic credit or
other form of academic advancement, including competitions and work placement.
Artificial intelligence7 (AI) is the ability of a machine to display human like capabilities such
as reasoning, learning, planning and creativity.

Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI)8 is the use or study of artificial intelligences that
are able to produce text, images, music, video, audio, code, or other synthetic data.
Unauthorised Content Generation (UCG)9 is defined as the production of academic work, in

competition; or placement interview, whether a payment or other favour is involved, using

Academic Fraud is defined as actions that are intended to deceive for unfair advantage by
violating academic regulations. This includes using deception to gain academic recognition.
Assessment refers to all work submitted for the purpose of gaining academic credit.
Work includes all assignments submitted to gain academic recognition, whether in written,
graphic, video, code, art, or any other relevant format as required.
Definitions above have been adapted from the NAIN Academic Integrity: National Principles
and Lexicon of Common Terms (2021)10.

6. Policy
SETU upholds the principle of academic integrity, whereby acknowledgement is given to the
contributions of others, which includes substantive contribution of technology, including
generative artificial intelligence in any work, through appropriate internal citations and
references.
6.1

SETU expects all students to uphold academic integrity and all work submitted by
students for assessment purposes, or for written and/or oral publication, must be
their own work. It is the student’s responsibility to use the required academic
integrity guidance and follow lecturers’ instructions.

6.2

SETU supports a culture of academic integrity and embeds academic integrity in
policies and procedures.

6.3

To support delivery of programmes, SETU provides academic integrity professional
development. This includes:
6.3.1

The provision by programme boards/streams of specific guidance to
students in relation to programme-related activities to support and develop
academic integrity and professional ethics within the discipline;

6.3.2

The review and redesign (where appropriate) of assessment schedules,
briefs, and rubrics, to support academic integrity;

6.4

Prior to the submission of assessments, students shall be briefed on academic practice

Tools to detect artificial intelligence generated content may be used to assist the

academic integrity and good academic practice. Upon assessment submission, any
student may be called upon to engage in an informal discussion and/or oral
examination in order to assist the lecturer in forming that academic judgement.

alleged instances of academic misconduct in accordance with the SETU Student
Discipline and Academic Misconduct Policy.11

Appendix 1: Use of Text-Matching Software (Turnitin)
A.1 Turnitin Text-Matching Software (or other university approved software) may be used:
•

formatively as part of the teaching of good academic practice

•

to deter potential plagiarists and to act as an incentive to following good academic practice

•

to help examiners to detect the extent and source of plagiarised material in work
submitted for assessment.

A.2 SETU acknowledges that students need support in interpreting and addressing feedback from
Turnitin Similarity Reports. Supporting resources shall be made available to students through the
relevant Faculty and/or functional area.
A.3 SETU recognises that students shall have the opportunity to self- submit a draft of their work to
Turnitin. The Turnitin Similarity Reports may help students to identify poorly referenced and
plagiarised work before submitting for assessment.
A.4 Irrespective of the Turnitin Similarity Report, the lecturer shall make the academic judgment as
to whether or not work is plagiarised.
A.5 Lecturers who use Turnitin shall:
A.5.1

Participate in (where feasible) appropriate professional development, e.g. a Turnitin
workshop, and/or access related resources, as provided by the University.

A.5.2

Use Turnitin as part of a holistic approach to fostering academic integrity among students.

A.5.3

Inform students in advance of the use of Turnitin for assignments and that assessment
data may be stored on the Turnitin database.

A.5.4

Allow students at least one draft submission with access to their Similarity Report before
final submission.

A.6 Turnitin is not a document repository and thus student assignments may not be accessible in
subsequent academic years. In the case where students have only submitted assessments
electronically, SETU shall retain the assignments as required by the SETU Record Retention
Schedule.
A.7 Through the act of submission to Turnitin, a student consents to the storage of their submission
in the Turnitin database. SETU may also upload student work to Turnitin; in the absence of a
student’s consent to the storing of their work in the Turnitin database, SETU will upload the
student work in a manner so that the student work is not added to the Turnitin database.
SETU is committed to protecting the rights and privacy of individuals with respect to the processing
of their personal data.
A.8 If a student submits a paper at SETU or elsewhere, and it indicates a high similarity
percentage to another paper, either within SETU or elsewhere, a View Request can
be made by the lecturer to request to view the matching paper. View Requests
received from within SETU may be considered by the lecturer. View Requests
emanating from outside the University shall be referred to the Vice President for
Academic Affairs or their nominee for consideration in accordance with the SETU
Student Discipline and Academic Misconduct Policy.14
